What the PID terms do

P · Proportional

Responds to the current error. More proportional gain usually drives the process toward the setpoint faster, but too much can cause overshoot or oscillation.

I · Integral

Accumulates error over time. It can remove steady-state offset, but excessive integral action may cause overshoot and slow recovery.

D · Derivative

Responds to how quickly error is changing. It can add damping and reduce overshoot in this idealized model; real derivative action is sensitive to measurement noise.

How to read the metrics and model

Overshoot is the largest amount the process rises above the setpoint, shown as a percent of the setpoint step. Settling time is the first time after which the process stays within ±2% of the step size around the setpoint until the simulation ends. Final error is setpoint minus process value at the end.

The sandbox uses a simple first-order-plus-dead-time (FOPDT) process: gain scales the delayed controller output, the time constant sets the lag speed, and dead time delays the process response. Real HVAC and industrial systems can include nonlinearities, actuator saturation, noise, hysteresis, multiple time constants, changing loads, and safety constraints that are not represented here.

Use the presets as starting points for learning only. Always follow validated procedures and equipment guidance when tuning real systems.
