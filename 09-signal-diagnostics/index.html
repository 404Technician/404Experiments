What signal diagnostics tell you

4–20 mA live zero

A nonzero 4 mA lower endpoint leaves room below the normal live-zero signal to indicate a very low current, such as a possible open loop or transmitter fault. This can make current-loop diagnostics useful, but a status alone cannot identify the cause.

Voltage input

0–10 V diagnostics here compare the reading with the configured signal range only. Voltage fault detection is generally less standardized than 4–20 mA loop diagnostics and depends on the input hardware and wiring.

Check device limits

Exact fault limits vary by transmitter, PLC, controller, input module, and configuration. The default thresholds are examples, not universal specifications. Consult the equipment documentation before using them operationally.

Examples

  • 4–20 mA: 12 mA is 50% of span; with a 0–100 °C range, it represents 50 °C.
  • Possible low-current fault: 3.5 mA is below the example 3.6 mA threshold. Actual fault interpretation depends on the connected devices.
  • 0–10 V: 5 V is 50% of span; outside 0–10 V, the engineering result is extrapolated and marked as a warning.
Calculated values outside the configured range are extrapolated, not clamped. Diagnostics are guidance and do not replace equipment specifications or electrical checks.
